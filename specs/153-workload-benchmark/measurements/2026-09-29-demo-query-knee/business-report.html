Bottom line. On this demo tenant, knowledge questions were answered successfully in every sample we ran. A typical new question finishes in about 7–14 seconds. If the same question is asked again, the system can answer in about 1–3 seconds by reusing a cached answer. We did not find a breaking point (capacity “knee”) up to four people asking at once under a 25-second comfort target.

1. What we tested (and why)

EdgeQuake is a knowledge system: documents are turned into a searchable graph, then people ask questions in natural language. Because answers depend on large language models (LLMs), wall-clock “requests per second” alone can mislead. This snapshot follows SPEC-153: measure the question-and-answer path on a named tenant, separate fresh answers from cached repeats, and speak in outcomes a business owner can use.

We exercised the live demo API with authenticated calls scoped to the tenant and workspace above. Workload shape: interactive Q&A over the papers already loaded in the workspace (including text-to-SQL and agentic research PDFs).

Important nuance. When we raised concurrency to 2 and 4 users, nearly every response was served from the answer cache (the model did not regenerate). That is excellent for repeat FAQs, but it is not the same as proving how many brand-new LLM answers the system can produce at once. We therefore do not publish an inflated “800+ tokens per second capacity” figure from that window.

3. Where the seconds go (fresh answer)

On unique follow-up questions, average time inside the service broke down roughly as follows:

StagePlain EnglishAvg timeShare
Understand the question Embed the query and extract search keywords ~2.8 s
Find evidence Search the graph and documents in the workspace ~0.9 s
Write the answer Language model generates the response ~4.2 s

In business terms: more than half of a fresh answer is waiting on the language model. Retrieval in this workspace is comparatively quick. Improving “time to answer” for new questions is mostly an LLM / prompt-budget topic, not a “database is slow” topic — for this corpus size.

4. Capacity under light concurrent use

We gradually increased simultaneous questioners (1 → 2 → 4) while requiring answers within a 25-second comfort target and a usable response every time.

Result: success stayed at 100%. We never hit a “breaking point” in this range. That supports a cautious statement: the demo tenant comfortably handled at least four concurrent interactive asks under today’s conditions — especially when some asks hit cache.

5. What this means for stakeholders

For product / customer success

  • Demo Q&A is reliable on this tenant for the loaded research corpus.
  • Set expectations: first answer ≈ 10 seconds; repeat FAQ ≈ a couple of seconds.
  • This workspace uses Mistral Small for answers (not the fleet OpenAI default shown on the public health page).

For engineering / capacity planning

  • Publish cold and warm numbers separately (SPEC-153 rule).
  • A true saturation knee for fresh LLM answers still needs a longer cold-only sweep (and optionally a dedicated bench tenant).
  • Ingest / PDF conversion capacity was intentionally not stressed on this shared demo.

Safe claims you can make today

  • Demo knowledge Q&A worked in 100% of sampled asks.
  • Fresh answers typically complete in about 7–14 seconds.
  • Cached repeats are about 1–3 seconds.
  • No failures observed up to four concurrent users under a 25-second target in this run.

Claims to avoid

  • Do not cite “800+ tokens/s system capacity” from the concurrent window — that mixed cache hits.
  • Do not claim a measured maximum capacity ceiling; we did not reach one.
  • Do not equate fleet health “OpenAI” with this workspace’s answer model (Mistral).
